  Three things point away from your two candidates:

  - The fit residual does not track the error. Spearman |error| vs ICP
    nn-residual is +0.10 / +0.03 / −0.03. Outliers or a ragged mask would
    inflate the residual on exactly the bad frames — they don't. The
    registration looks healthy while being wrong, which is the signature of a
    wrong correspondence, not a noisy one.
  - The leftover is drift, not jitter. Splitting at 1 s: axial drift 4.8° vs

    points (−0.24 / −0.31 / −0.29). So: more visible torso → better; further
    from neutral → worse.

  That is self-occlusion. As the trunk turns, the camera sees a different patch
  of skin than the neutral cloud holds, and ICP slides that patch onto the old
  one — under-reading the rotation (slope < 1) and accumulating a slow offset.
  Worst in axial, where the shell is closest to a surface of revolution.

  shift.

  Three caveats

  Matched fraction is useless as a gate. It keeps 97–100 % of frames and changes
  nothing (gokul axial 14.3 → 13.8° at ≥0.85). It saturates near 0.95 even when
  occluded, because ICP matches the points it still has — it cannot know about
  the surface that left the view. Correlation with real coverage is only
  +0.50…+0.71. So the gate needs the KD query against the neutral cloud; one
  query per frame, cheap.

  The cost is exactly the range you care about. Surviving mocap p95 |rotation|
  drops from 37→26° (kabil), 37→23° (gokul), 59→25° (js), 64→25° (diwakar). The
  gate defines a measurable envelope of roughly ±25–30°, and every RMSE then has
  to be quoted with its duty cycle. For js that duty cycle is 51 % — half the
  take unreported.

  It does not rescue ram. Gating at 0.65 leaves it unchanged (flexion r −0.01,
  axial r −0.18); at 0.80 the numbers drop but flexion correlation goes
  negative. Its problem is the frame assignment, not occlusion — consistent with
  the 132° S disagreement. It stays excluded.
