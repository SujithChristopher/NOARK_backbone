Synthetic people for unseen-subject trunk tracking

Can rendered people teach a small on-device pose model to read the trunk of people it has never seen? Five model versions, 30,000 synthetic frames, scored against the teacher model and against motion capture.

Period 16–19 Oct 2026 Repos NOARK_backbone (branch diwakar_val), minisam3d Test subjects arjun, jerome, lee (never trained on) Status complete; v3mix recipe is the current best

1. Summary

Key findings

  1. The problem was generalisation, not capacity. Against the teacher (per-take zeroed), the baseline student is at 1.1° on its training people and 3.2° on new people. On new people it also under-reads forward/back lean (flexion gain 0.74 instead of 0.95).
  2. The teacher reads synthetic flexion badly. On rendered people SAM 3D Body recovers only 54–58 % of the true forward/back lean. Twist and side-bend are fine (gain 0.92–0.98), and on real people it reads lean correctly against mocap. So synthetic trunk labels use the rendered truth, not the teacher.
  3. Synthetic people help forward/back lean, robustly. Flexion error against mocap fell from 2.45° (no synthetic data) to 1.82–2.02° in all four synthetic runs. Overall trunk angle error fell from 2.85° to 2.49–2.76°.
  4. Synthetic people also reduced the shrinkage of large lean, but only part-way. On new people, the baseline reads 5–20° of lean at 0.67–0.72 of the teacher's value; the synthetic runs at 0.76–0.84.
  5. Beyond that, re-weighting the mix and balancing lean added little or nothing. Raising synthetic frames to 40 % of each batch (v3mix) gained 0.1–0.3° against mocap, about the run-to-run noise. A second set with lean spread evenly from −10° to +40° (v4mix) changed nothing: shrinkage stayed at about 0.82.
  6. Two runs of one recipe differ by up to 0.18° on the six-take averages and by about 0.5° per person. With one run per arm, differences below about 0.2° are not results.
  7. The remaining gap to the teacher sits in two places. Every student under-reads Jerome's movement (overall 2.4–3.0° vs the teacher's 1.35°), and every student reads Lee's twist 4–6° too high in the lee_wc take (teacher: +1.8°). Arjun is at teacher level.
  8. Arms improved a little with synthetic data: wrist error 60.7 → 55–58 mm, keypoints 4.61 → 4.2–4.5 px.

Current best: the v3mix recipe (r18_v3mix_all and its repeat r18_v3mix_all_rep2). Against mocap it averages 1.8° flexion and 2.5–2.7° overall trunk angle on the three unseen people. The teacher reaches 1.5° and 2.1°, depth ICP 1.4° and 1.9°.

2. Background

The student

NOARK measures trunk compensation while a person plays a reaching game at a table. A camera on the table looks at the seated person. The reference perception model is SAM 3D Body (the teacher, ViT-H backbone, MHR body model). It reads the trunk well but takes about 0.26 s per frame on a laptop GPU. The miniSAM3D student is a ResNet-18 distilled from it. Its input is a 256×256 grey crop from the mono OV9281 camera, and it outputs 14 upper-body keypoints (2D and 3D) plus the global rotations of 9 joints (trunk, clavicles, upper arms, forearms, wrists) as 6D rotations. It runs in about 4 ms on the Radxa NPU (W8A16).

The trunk is the global rotation of MHR joint c_spine3, the upper-thoracic segment where the mocap sternum rigid body sits.

The problem: it learned the people, not the pose

The baseline student, r18_v2_all, matches the teacher closely on the five people it was trained on, and much less on new people. The gap is not a capacity limit: on its training takes the same network agrees with the teacher to about 1°. It is identity: 5 real people supply 63 % of training frames, and the network partly memorises them.

The obvious fix is more people, and recording enough real people is slow. Rendering them is cheap. This log covers that attempt.

Axis convention

Rotations are compared in OpenCV camera axes (x right, y down, z away from the camera). A trunk rotation relative to a reference is written as a rotation vector. Its components are:

  • flexion = x: forward/back lean. Positive leans toward the camera, which is forward over the table.
  • axial = y: twist about the vertical axis.
  • lateral = z: side bend, in the image plane.

MHR's global rotations use the body model's y-up axes, and are conjugated by diag(1, −1, −1) into camera axes. Relative rotations in camera axes do not depend on how a skeleton defines its bone frames, so student, teacher, render and mocap can be compared directly once each is zeroed.

Forward/back lean is the hard axis for a single camera: it moves the chest toward the lens, so the image changes mainly through scale and foreshortening. Side bend is an in-plane rotation and easy to see.

4. Evaluation method

Two independent references, each with its own blind spot.

4.1 Against the teacher (all 9 test takes, 19,305 frames)

This shows how well the student imitates its teacher on new people. It covers game takes too, which have no mocap match.

  • abs: median angle between student and teacher trunk rotations.
  • zeroed: each side is first made relative to its own take mean (chordal mean), then the median angle between them. This removes the constant per-take offset, which live use removes anyway because a session starts from a neutral pose. About half of abs on new people is such an offset.
  • flex / axial / lat: median |difference| of the zeroed rotation-vector components.
  • gain: least-squares slope of student on teacher for each component. A gain below 1 means the student reads smaller movements than the teacher.
  • Keypoints and arms (eval_teacher.py, frame-weighted):
    • px: 2D keypoint error in the 256-px crop.
    • mm: mean 3D keypoint error.
    • wrist / elbow: 3D error of those joints, left and right averaged.
    • forearm: geodesic rotation error.

Takes are averaged with equal weight ("mean per take").

4.2 Against motion capture (6 standard-movement takes)

This is the ground truth that matters. Scoring is NOARK's 21_sam3d_score.py, run unchanged through score_student_noark.py. The protocol, per take:

  1. Hardware sync: a GPIO pulse ties camera frames to mocap time; no cross-correlation guessing.
  2. Only the longest mocap segment without rigid-body "teleports" (Motive re-lock jumps) is used.
  3. Both systems are zeroed on the mean of their first 15 common valid frames.
  4. The residual rotation between the camera and mocap frames (S) is fitted per method against mocap.
  5. Only frames where depth ICP also solved are scored, so every method sees exactly the same frames.
  6. For each take, the median |error| per axis and for the total angle (magnitude) is computed. The summary is the mean of the six per-take medians.

The teacher's and ICP's numbers use the same frames and the same protocol.

Caveats.
  • S and the zeroing use mocap, which live use doesn't have. Absolute numbers are therefore optimistic for every method alike, and comparisons between methods remain fair.
  • Game takes are not scored against mocap.
  • Six takes from three people is a small test set (see run-to-run noise).

4.3 Shrinkage analysis

On the unseen standard-movement takes, frames are binned by the teacher's |zeroed flexion| (0–5, 5–10, 10–20, ≥20°). Per bin, the median of student/teacher is the ratio: 1 means right amplitude, below 1 means the student shrinks the movement toward upright. Lateral bend is the control axis.

4.4 Run-to-run noise

train.py seeds only the 2 % validation split. Batch sampling, augmentation and the new rotation head's initialisation are not seeded, so rerunning an identical command gives an independent run on identical data. One such repeat was trained (§6.5).

5. Synthetic people

Code in minisam3d/synth/ (README there). Two sets were made, each 1,500 people × 10 poses.

5.1 Poses: real, from training people only

build_pose_pool.py collects the teacher's fits on all training-subject takes: 51,992 seated poses. Each pose is stored as joint-rotation deltas from that take's neutral (pelvis, spine, neck, head, clavicles) plus the arm keypoints. A seated range-of-motion filter drops implausible fits: spine vs pelvis flexion −25…70°, |twist| and |side bend| ≤ 45°.

Retargeting onto a synthetic body works as follows:

  • The pelvis turns the whole body and the chair, since the office chair swivels.
  • Spine, neck, head and clavicles copy the deltas.
  • The arms aim along the real shoulder→elbow→wrist→knuckle keypoints, with the elbow hinge rolled into the real arm plane and the forearm twist taken from the real palm.

The rendered trunk then equals the teacher's delta to < 0.06°.

5.2 People, scenes, camera

  • People: random adult MPFB2 (MakeHuman) humans: build, age (about 19–63), sex, skin, hair, outfit. All assets are CC0. The outfit pack has only about 10 full-body outfits, so clothing diversity is the weakest axis.
  • Scenes:
    • Background: an indoor HDRI (half the time) or a box room with photo-textured or painted walls and a window.
    • Table: laminate or wood veneer.
    • Extras: clutter; a bystander (20 %); the AprilTag robot (always for game poses, otherwise 40 %).
  • Camera: the oct rig's upright pinhole at half size (640×400), 3–6 cm above the table, pitch −3…6°, yaw ±3°.
  • Render: Blender 5.1 Cycles, 16 samples with OptiX denoising, 3 Blender processes in parallel on the RTX 4060 laptop. Throughput was 2.7–3.0 frames/s: 1.4–1.6 h per 15k set.

5.3 The camera look

Renders are clean RGB, and the rig is a noisy, fisheye-undistorted near-infrared mono camera. export_sam3d.py applies the look once, so the teacher labels the same grey image the student trains on. The steps, with per-person or per-frame random parameters:

  1. Grey: luma blended toward a red-heavy, NIR-like mix.
  2. Tone: gain and offset put the person box at the real takes' mean (100–155) and spread (45–65).
  3. Highlights: a soft tanh shoulder, because renders clip the white table and real frames rarely do.
  4. Finish: vignette, Gaussian blur (σ 0.3–1.0) and sensor noise (σ 1.5–4).

Histogram-matching to real frames was tried and rejected: it posterised the person into a flat silhouette.

5.4 The teacher under-reads synthetic lean

Each synthetic frame went through the teacher, like a real take: 15,000 of 15,000 frames solved, at 0.26–0.37 s/frame. The renders carry the exact trunk rotation, so the teacher can be scored directly. Each side is zeroed per person (the 10 poses of one group).

Three checks say the render is right and the teacher is wrong:

  • On the rendered body itself, the neck–pelvis line follows the label at gain 0.86–0.94.
  • Twist and side bend are read nearly perfectly.
  • On real people the teacher's flexion matches mocap at gain about 0.9.

This is a domain effect of the teacher on rendered people. So the synthetic trunk label is the rendered truth, aligned per person to the teacher's convention. gt_trunk.py sets label = Rbone·X, where X is the per-person chordal mean of RboneTRteacher. Relative motion therefore follows the render, and the absolute frame stays the teacher's. Keypoints and arm rotations keep the teacher labels. Label vs teacher: median 5.3°.

6.1 r18_v2_all — baseline

Data: noark_v2, 28 roots. It has every training-subject take from both sessions, including wc and game, plus COCO 2014: 67k frames from 5 real people.

Why it is the baseline: it was the best deployable model before this work. Against mocap it averages flexion 2.45°, overall 2.85°, with constant offsets on some takes (Lee's wc twist +5.8°, Jerome's wc flexion −4.5°).

6.2 r18_v3_all — add 1,500 synthetic people

Why: §2. New identities are the lever.

Change: noark_v3 = v2 + syn15k (15,000 frames). Synthetic frames are drawn at their natural share (18 % of batches).

Result: against mocap, flexion improved 2.45 → 2.02° and twist 2.82 → 2.33°; side bend got worse (2.07 → 2.64°). Against the teacher almost nothing moved (zeroed 3.15 → 3.08°). Keypoints and arms improved about 3 %.

6.3 r18_v3mix_all — give the synthetic people more weight

Why: with natural sampling, the 5 real people still filled 63 % of every batch and the 1,500 synthetic people only 18 %. If identity memorisation is the problem, the batches should be dominated by many identities.

Change: a new --mix option in train.py fixes each group's share of the draws (weighted sampling with replacement, same epoch length): COCO 15 %, real 45 %, synthetic 40 %. Each synthetic frame is then seen 2.2× per epoch and each real frame 0.71×.

Result: best on most metrics. Against the teacher, zeroed 2.84° and flexion 1.57°. Against mocap, flexion 1.85° and overall 2.67°. The second run (§6.5) shows that part of the step over v3 is within noise.

6.4 r18_v4mix_all — spread the lean evenly

Why: on new people the student shrinks large lean toward upright. The ratio to the teacher was about 1.0 below 5° and about 0.8 at 5–20°, while side bend was never shrunk. The training lean is also mostly near-upright: 72–85 % of real frames and 71 % of syn15k are within ±5°, against only 51 % in the test protocol takes. A model unsure on a new person falls back to that prior.

Change: synflex15k, 15,000 more frames with 1,500 new people (seed 1).

  • Pose weighting: flex_pool.py re-weights the same real pose pool so that lean is even over −10…40° in 5° bins. No pose is used more than 10× on average. The pool has only 114 poses at 30–35° and 22 at 35–40°, so those bins got 1,106 and 234 frames.
  • Mirroring: half the poses are mirrored left↔right. The student's flip augmentation carries no rotation loss, so mirrored renders add labelled variety.
  • Training: noark_v4 with the v3mix shares and --epoch-len 80386, so the step count matches v3mix exactly and only the synthetic content differs.

Result: no measurable change. Against mocap, flexion 1.84° vs 1.85°. Shrinkage at 5–20° stayed at about 0.82, and side bend got 0.25° worse. Against the teacher it was 0.2° worse, which is within noise. The teacher again under-read this set's lean (gain 0.58).

6.5 r18_v3mix_all_rep2 — how noisy is one run?

Why: every comparison so far was one run per arm, and differences of 0.1–0.3° could be chance.

Change: none. The v3mix command was rerun as-is. Claude Code's low-memory guard killed the run three times; it was resumed from last.pt each time (optimizer and schedule state restored). The loader workers were cut 10 → 6 → 4, which changes speed only.

Result: §7.5. The two runs differ by up to 0.18° on the six-take means and about 0.5° per person.

Possible cause for Lee: the wc and game recordings differ in brightness by 2.3× (torso mean intensity 74 vs 168). The student sees only the grey image, and the teacher handles it. For Jerome the student shrinks his movement, the same failure as §7.3, concentrated on one person.

7.5 Run-to-run noise

Single takes move by up to about 1° between the two runs (arjun_woc overall 2.8 vs 1.7°). The practical rule: with one run per arm, treat six-take differences below about 0.2°, and per-person differences below about 0.5°, as noise. To rank recipes closer than that, run each at least twice.

8. Discussion

What the synthetic people fixed

Forward/back lean against mocap improved by 0.4–0.6° in every synthetic run. That is the axis a single camera finds hardest, and the gain exceeds the noise by a wide margin. Arms and keypoints also improved slightly. It worked even though the teacher cannot label synthetic lean, because the trunk label came from the render.

What they did not fix

Shrinkage of 5–20° lean on new people improved from about 0.7 to about 0.82, then stopped. In v4mix, 75 % of the synthetic frames were outside ±5°, and it still did not move past 0.82. So beyond that point, a shortage of leaning examples is not the cause. The likely explanations, none of them tested yet:

  • Domain gap. The model may learn lean on synthetic appearance and fall back on the real-data prior for real appearance.
  • Weak depth cue. Lean shows mainly as scale and foreshortening, which a grey crop shows poorly and which varies with distance between people.
  • Identity variety is still narrow where it matters: about 10 outfits.

Where the remaining gap is

The gap to the teacher is not spread evenly; it is concentrated in Jerome (under-read movement) and Lee's wc take (twist offset). Both are consistent across all students and both repeat runs, so they are real properties of the student, and good targets.

Limits of this study

  • Small test set: three test people and six mocap-scored takes. One unusual person can move a mean.
  • Few repeats: one or two runs per recipe.
  • Mocap protocol fits S and zeroing with mocap, which live use cannot.
  • Game takes are scored only against the teacher.

9. Next steps

  1. Lee's twist bias: a cheap test first. Run the student on lee_wc with the frames brightened to game level. If the +5° twist offset moves, add a stronger brightness and gain augmentation to training.
  2. Jerome: compare his crops with the training people (clothing, build, distance). Find what the student keys on before adding data.
  3. Clothing variety in the renders, the weakest randomised axis (about 10 outfits), if the Jerome check points to appearance.
  4. Any further recipe comparison: at least two runs per arm, launched detached (Appendix D).

D. Engineering notes

  • Rendering: 3 parallel Blender processes beat one. The GPU part of a 640×400, 16-sample frame is small, and the rest of the work is single-threaded. OptiX denoising and uncompressed PNG halved the per-frame time.
  • Windows DataLoader: workers are spawned, so analysis scripts piped through stdin hang. Use a script file with if __name__ == "__main__". Workers re-spawn every epoch, about 30 s.
  • Memory: each training worker holds about 2 GB, the main process about 6.5 GB. Claude Code's low-memory guard killed background training three times on the 32 GB laptop. Long runs are now launched as a detached process with 4 workers.
  • Sleep: the laptop slept for 2 h mid-run once. The process paused and continued correctly.
