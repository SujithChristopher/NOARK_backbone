NOARK device tracking · validation log

Dual-camera tag jig against motion capture

How well does Diwakar's two-camera, eight-tag tracker locate the NOARK device on the table? Two runs of a 96-point grid, twelve moving takes and three jig-calibration takes, scored against an OptiTrack Motive rigid body.

Key findings

  1. The recorded pipeline is reproduced exactly. Re-running Diwakar's public tracker on the recorded fisheye crops gives his logged corners to a median of 0.004 px. His stereo solver on those corners gives his logged poses to 0.5 µm, and his mocap fit gives his published numbers exactly (0.309 / 0.629 / 0.330 mm across / depth / height, run 1).
  2. His published numbers are optimistic, for two reasons. They are in-sample, and the fit includes a free scale on the camera translation (0.992). Fitted on one run and tested on the other, they stay close (0.34–0.39 / 0.63–0.77 mm). With the scale fixed at 1 they become 0.73–0.80 / 0.72–1.0 mm. The scale is the same in both runs, so it is a real camera range error of about 0.7 %, not fitting noise.
  3. The mocap reference was steady. In 176 of 193 grid holds the mocap rigid body moved less than its own noise (median 3D SD 0.05 mm). Seventeen holds settled or were nudged, by at most 1.3 mm. That is too small to explain the 1–1.5 mm hold errors.
  4. The rigid jig is what makes the tracker accurate. Solving all visible tags as one rigid body, instead of tag 12 alone, halves the static error (T1, stereo: 1.1 vs 2.7 mm). It also cuts jitter 2–5× and raises coverage from 87 % to 100 %.
  5. Corner refinement matters as much as the jig. Diwakar's edge refinement and rapidtag's AprilTag refinement are tied (T1 stereo 1.48 / 1.51 mm). Plain OpenCV corners (2.16 mm) and OpenCV CONTOUR refinement (2.32 mm) are clearly worse, and CONTOUR is worse than no refinement.
  6. Jitter is at the mocap floor. With edge-refined corners, the jig and both cameras, the hold-still jitter is 0.053 mm, against 0.049 mm for the mocap itself. Unrefined corners give 0.31 mm.
  7. Undistorting the image or only the corners gives the same accuracy. Undistorting first lowers jitter by 5–20 % for threshold-based refiners and makes no difference for edge refinement.
  8. Moving error is 1.2–2.8 mm and grows with speed. The registration is frozen from the grid. Comfortable reaching gives 1.2 mm, slow shapes 1.5 mm and fast shapes 2.8 mm; peaks come at direction changes. The GPIO sync shows zero lag in every take.

Recommended configuration: both cameras solved jointly, all tags as one rigid jig, and edge-refined or AprilTag-refined corners. This is the configuration Diwakar shipped. When reporting accuracy, use a registration fitted on one run and tested on the other, with no free scale. On that basis the device is accurate to about 1.1–1.4 mm on the table plane when still and 1.2–2.8 mm when moving.

2. Background

The NOARK game reads the position of a hand-held device on the table. Diwakar built a tracker for it: two global-shutter cameras on a Radxa board look at an eight-tag AprilTag jig mounted on the device, at 100 fps. He recorded a validation set against an OptiTrack Motive system and reported sub-millimetre agreement on a grid of static positions.

This log answers four questions about that set:

  1. Can his result be reproduced from the raw recordings, and what exactly does it measure?
  2. What is the accuracy under an honest protocol: held-out registration and no free scale?
  3. Which parts of the pipeline matter: stereo vs one camera, rigid jig vs one tag, corner refinement, and the undistortion path?
  4. How large is the error when the device moves?

3. Setup and data

Hardware

Two OV9281-class global-shutter cameras (1280×800, fisheye lenses, 5 ms exposure, gain 4) sit on a stereo bar on the table and face the player. The 77 mm baseline cameras are calibrated with a 12×8 checkerboard (fisheye model, 0.12 px reprojection). Their stereo extrinsics are stored as p_cam0 = Rx·p_cam1 + tx. The device carries eight 50 mm AprilTag 36h11 tags (ids 4, 8, 12, 14, 20, 24, 28, 32) on a 3D-printed jig. Tag 12 faces the cameras and defines the jig frame. Four Motive markers on the device form the mocap rigid body noark, recorded at 100 Hz.

Takes

  • T0: free hand-held motion with no mocap, used to calibrate the jig by bundle adjustment. Only the 10-03 T0 takes have images.
  • T1: the grid. The device is placed on 96 table points (12 across × 8 deep, about 96 × 83 mm spacing) and held for about 3 s each. The operator marks hold_start/hold_end in marks.csv. In run 2 one point was held twice (holds 50/51); the redo is kept.
  • T2: circles and figure-eights at slow, comfortable and fast speed, two runs each.
  • T3: free reaching at comfortable and fast speed, three runs each.

Images are stored for every sample in T1, but only every second sample in T2/T3.

Conventions

  • Corners (corners.csv) are in the undistorted pinhole image of each camera: intrinsics meta['undistorted'], zero distortion, corner order TL, TR, BR, BL. This was checked against the poses in samples.csv (0.47 px reprojection).
  • Poses in samples.csv are the jig pose in cam0's frame from the joint stereo solve only. Single-camera poses had to be recomputed.
  • Mocap is Motive Y-up: X across the table, Y height, Z depth (toward or away from the cameras). The table plane is X–Z.
  • Sync: Motive emits one GPIO pulse per frame. The k-th rising edge in sync.csv is mocap frame k (10 ms, no gaps). Camera samples carry monotonic timestamps on the same clock as the edges.

5. Evaluation method

Holds

Each T1 hold is the span from hold_start to hold_end (about 3 s). Both ends are trimmed by 0.5 s to drop settling. The device value per hold is the median grip point over the camera samples inside it. The mocap value is the mean rigid-body position and orientation over the GPIO-synced frames inside it.

Registration (ours)

The camera and mocap frames are unrelated, and the mocap rigid body's origin is not the grip point. Per solver and corner source we fit a rigid transform plus an offset on the body:

p_mocap,i + R_body,i · g_body  =  R_wc · g_cam,i + t_wc        (9 parameters: R_wc, t_wc, g_body)

The fit uses one T1 run and is scored on the other (held out), both ways. There is no scale parameter. Errors are in mocap X (across) and Z (depth). Y (height) is nearly constant on the table and is not scored. For the moving takes, the registration fitted on both T1 runs is frozen and applied to each per-sample pose. Mocap is interpolated to the camera timestamps through the GPIO edges, and frames where the mocap jumps more than 20 mm in 10 ms (Motive re-acquisition) are dropped.

Registration (Diwakar's)

His fit has 13 parameters: camera→mocap rotation and translation, board→body rotation, the body origin in the board frame, and a free scale on the camera translation. It is fitted and scored on the same run, with position residuals in mm and rotation residuals weighted 200 mm/rad. Errors are expressed in his table frame: across ≈ −X, depth ≈ −Z, height ≈ Y.

Jitter

A still hold is a free jitter test. Each trimmed hold is cut into 50-frame windows (0.5 s, matching the dome burst protocol in jitter_model/). Hold jitter is the median window SD of the grip point, as a 3D norm in mocap axes. The mocap rigid body's SD over the same windows is the floor. All conditions are compared on the same frames: a frame counts only if every variant for that solver produced a pose.

6. Results

6.1 Was the jig still during the grid holds?

Every T1 number assumes the device did not move during a hold. Mocap can test this directly. For each hold (untrimmed), we computed the 3D SD of the rigid body about its hold mean, the frame-to-frame noise (SD of successive differences / √2), the largest deviation, the linear drift over the hold, and the largest rotation from the mean orientation.

Verdict: the jig was still in 176 of 193 holds. The median SD is 0.05 mm, the same as Motive's noise. The flagged holds moved by at most 1.3 mm. The worst are run 1 holds 6–7 (the first row, where the jig apparently was still settling) and the first attempt at run 2 hold 50, which was redone. These shifts are much smaller than the 1–1.5 mm hold errors in §6.2. They are also partly removed by the 0.5 s trim, so the reference is not the limiting factor.

6.2 Static accuracy on the grid

The table uses analyze_T1.py: corners re-solved with the T0-rebuilt jig, and joint (logged) = Diwakar's own poses with the shipped jig. All four land at 1.2–1.7 mm held out. Stereo is best in depth (Z), and his shipped solve is the best overall.

6.3 His metric against ours

Read across the logged row. In-sample with free scale (his protocol) gives 0.31 / 0.63 / 0.33 mm. Testing on the other run costs little (0.34–0.39 across). Fixing the scale at 1 doubles the across error to 0.73–0.80 mm, because a 0.77 % scale error over a 700 mm workspace is several millimetres. The fitted scale agrees between runs (0.9921 / 0.9937), so this is a real camera range bias. It most likely comes from the intrinsics or the stereo baseline, and recalibrating could remove it. His 13-parameter model also fits orientation and averages the whole untrimmed hold, which lowers RMSE further. Our 9-parameter position-only model without scale is stricter, and that explains why the same poses score 1.0–1.4 mm XZ in §6.2.

6.4 Corner refinement

The saved crops allow re-detection with other corner methods, using the same frames, jig rebuild and solvers:

  • logged: Diwakar's tracker (OpenCV CONTOUR, then his edge refinement: grey-level edge lines intersected).
  • april: rapidtag ≥ 0.1.12 with CORNER_REFINE_APRILTAG.
  • none: OpenCV quads, no refinement.
  • contour: OpenCV CORNER_REFINE_CONTOUR alone.

Re-detected corners differ from the logged ones by a median 0.75–0.93 px. The size difference is the more important one. Threshold corners (none, contour) make tags about 1.7–2.0 % smaller than edge-refined corners, while AprilTag corners match the edge-refined size (+0.04 %). A tag that looks smaller looks farther away, and with a jig built from the same corners this turns into the depth errors above.

6.5 Rigid jig against one tag

Tag 12 alone is visible in 87 % of samples; the jig gives a pose in 100 %. One 50 mm tag at 0.3–0.7 m constrains depth only through its apparent size. The jig spans about 160 × 230 mm, so depth comes from the spread between tags.

6.6 Static jitter

The SD over a whole hold is 2–3× the window SD, so a slow drift of 0.1–0.15 mm sits on top of the jitter. Part of it is the device settling (§6.1), and part is the estimator.

6.7 Undistort the image, or only the corners?

The tracker remaps every frame from fisheye to pinhole and then detects tags. The alternative is to detect on the fisheye image and undistort only the corner points (cv2.fisheye.undistortPoints), which is cheaper. detect_paths.py ran both paths with identical detector settings and four refiners. Each combination got its own T0 jig.

Accuracy is a tie: within 0.04 mm for edge and AprilTag refinement. Undistorting first lowers jitter by 5–20 % for the threshold refiners (none, contour) and about 5 % for AprilTag. For edge refinement it changes nothing. Detecting on the fisheye image is a safe way to save the per-frame remap.

6.8 Moving tasks

A constant lag would show up as error proportional to velocity. The lag scan found 0 ms in every take, so the speed dependence is not a timing offset. Likely sources are motion blur at 5 ms exposure (about 2 mm of travel at 0.4 m/s), the up-to-5 ms skew between the two cameras' exposures, and steeper tag angles during fast turns.

7. Discussion and limits

  • What the sub-millimetre number means. It is correct for what it measures: in-sample agreement after fitting a scale. As a deployment claim, the honest figure is about 1.1–1.4 mm static and 1.2–2.8 mm moving. The game needs centimetre-level positions, so either figure is ample.
  • The 0.7 % range error is the largest fixable error. It is consistent between runs and shows up as a depth-proportional Z residual. Rechecking the stereo baseline and focal lengths, for example against a measured bar on the table, should bring the no-scale error close to the free-scale figure.
  • The reference is good enough. The mocap rigid body was still to 0.05 mm and its jitter is the same as the tracker's. Below about 0.05 mm the validation cannot separate tracker from mocap.
  • Only one device and one session. All takes are one jig, one camera pair, one day (plus the 10-05 T0). The numbers do not cover a recalibrated or rebuilt device.
  • Height (Y) and orientation are not scored. The device slides on a flat table, so Y and tilt barely vary.
  • Moving-task registration depends on T1. T2/T3 errors include any registration error carried over from the grid. That error is small (held-out T1 ≈ in-sample T1) but not zero.

8. Next steps

  1. Recalibrate or verify the stereo scale (a known-length bar, or a checkerboard at several depths), then re-score T1 without scale.
  2. Use the stereo + jig + edge/AprilTag configuration as the reference tracker. If the remap costs CPU on the Radxa, switch to fisheye-space detection, since §6.7 found no accuracy cost.
  3. For fast motion, test a shorter exposure (2–3 ms with more gain) and per-camera timestamp interpolation in the stereo solve.
  4. Report validation numbers with held-out registration and no free scale. Report the free-scale figure only alongside it.
